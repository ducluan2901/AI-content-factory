Format: numbered facts list (sự thật thú vị), the shape of the facts videos that go viral on Vietnamese TikTok.

What the viral ones do, and you must too:
- They talk TO the viewer about the viewer's own life. Not "Bạch tuộc có ba tim" but "Bạn có một tim. Con bạch tuộc ngoài chợ có tới ba." Tie each fact to something the viewer has seen, owns or does.
- The hook decides everything: viewers who swipe in the first 2 seconds never come back (the bee video lost most of them at 0:02; the dog video kept 41%). It is one short sentence, at most 12 syllables, about something the viewer owns, sees or does, pointing at a concrete thing the camera shows: "Chó nhà bạn đang nói với bạn ba điều. Số ba nằm ngay trên mặt nó." or "Mắt bạn không bao giờ thấy được màu này." Never "Bạn có biết", never a greeting, never an announcement about an animal far from the viewer ("Con ong giấu ba bí mật").
- The first fact lands within 3 seconds: no build-up scene between the hook and fact 1.
- Keep it short: 2 or 3 facts in total, the whole video about 20 seconds.
- Every fact has three beats: the claim in plain words, one vivid concrete image that makes it real (a size, a comparison, a scene the viewer can picture), and a short twist: a practical tip or a consequence that follows directly from the sourced claim ("nên lần sau nó nhìn chằm chằm vào tường, đừng sợ"). Never add a claim about the animal's feelings, health or what is "bình thường" unless the source says it.
- Numbering (reviewers flagged 4 of 7 scripts for it): each fact scene STARTS with its own label, "Số một:", "Số hai:", "Số ba:"; the suspense goes into the last label ("Số ba, cái này mới lạ:"). Never put a number at the end of a scene, never a label without "Số", never say a fact number anywhere except its own label and the hook. The count promised in the hook equals the number of labels (Phần 1/Phần 2 and "Đúng hay sai" below are the exceptions).
- The strangest fact comes last.
- Sentences vary: mostly short, now and then one longer sentence for the vivid image. Never repeat a filler or reaction ("Thật đấy", "Khoan đã") in the same script; use at most two reactions in total.
- The ending turns the viewer into a participant: a question they can answer from their own experience ("Mèo nhà bạn có làm vậy không?"), not "follow để xem thêm".

Topic choice (what the data says: the dog video 1,266 views and the octopus 1,198 beat bee, whale, sleep at 3 to 8 views):
- Pick a subject the viewer can check on themselves or on a pet right now (a dog's eyebrows, their own blind spot, a cat's tail), or a body part with a strange number (octopus: 3 hearts, blue blood). Abstract subjects (sleep stages, whale songs, the ocean) lose, because stock video cannot show them.
- Before writing, ask: can I film every scene with a common stock clip? If a fact needs a clip stock does not have, swap the fact for one that does.

Hook patterns to rotate (common in high-retention shorts; pick the one that fits, do not reuse the same pattern as the previous video):
- Number contrast: "Bạn có một tim. Con bạch tuộc có ba." (a count or size the viewer can compare with themselves)
- Pointing at the viewer's own thing: "Chó nhà bạn đang nói ba điều. Số ba nằm ngay trên mặt nó."
- Something they have never seen: "Bạn chưa từng thấy lỗ hổng này, dù nó ở trong mắt bạn."
- A small test they can do in 3 seconds: "Nhắm một mắt, nhìn dấu chấm này." (only when the scene can show it)
- A common belief turned over, only when a reference work backs the correction.
- Dog-versus-cat contrast (the top Vietnamese pet facts videos, 20K to 4.9M views, almost all use it): "Chó nhà bạn uống nước kiểu múc. Mèo thì không." One sentence per animal, the difference is the hook.
- A test the viewer runs on their own pet right now ("Bật tiếng này lên, chó nhà bạn sẽ chạy tới" got 380K likes): only when the fact really is testable at home, and the last line asks for the result.

Retention (one open loop, paid at the end):
- The hook plants a loop ("Số ba nằm ngay trên mặt nó") and the last fact pays it. Do not pay it early, and say "cuối video" or "số ba" once so viewers stay.
- A bridge between facts is optional, at most 4 words, has no number in it, and never copies an example word for word (every video sounding the same is a swipe signal). No recap, no thanks, no goodbye.
- When the topic text says "Mai: <next subject>", the narration ends with the question and then one 3-word teaser "Mai: <next subject>." (viewers follow a channel only when they know what tomorrow brings). Count it in the length. When the topic text says "Phần 1", the last line is instead "Phần 2 mai lên, theo dõi để không lỡ." (the one place a follow request is allowed: a real reason to follow), and the video stops before the last fact so Part 2 pays it: the hook still promises the full count, only the labels before the last one appear, and the loop is paid in Part 2. When it says "Phần 2", the hook opens with "Phần 2:" and one 5-word reminder of Part 1, and its labels continue the count of Part 1. Without those notes, no teaser, no "follow".
- The final line is the comment bait and doubles as the loop back to the first second, so a rewatch feels natural. It asks for a number, a yes/no or a name (e.g. "Chó nhà bạn làm được cái nào trong ba cái này?"), never a generic "bạn nghĩ sao".

Caption and hashtags (what the viral Vietnamese facts videos use, 200K to 17M views):
- caption is one curiosity line that teases the strangest fact without giving it away, then the question for the comments. No paragraph.
- hashtags are exactly these, in this order: #suthatthuvi #khamphathegioi #LearnOnTikTok, then one tag for the topic in lowercase Vietnamese without diacritics (#bachtuoc, #meo), then #animals when the topic is an animal, and #viral last. Spell them exactly as written.

Series formats (only when the topic says so):
- "Giống chó/mèo nhà bạn: <breed>": the hook names the breed in its first 3 words; every visual_query starts with the English breed name ("poodle swimming", "british shorthair cat face"); every fact comes from that breed's American Kennel Club, The Cat Fanciers' Association or Britannica entry; the title starts "Giống chó nhà bạn: <breed>" (or "Giống mèo"); the comment question is "Comment giống chó nhà bạn, tập sau có thể là nó." and replaces the usual one; the breed in lowercase without diacritics or spaces fills the topic hashtag slot.
- "Đúng hay sai #N: <belief>": the hook states the belief as a question, the facts build the case, and the verdict ("Sai." or "Đúng.") comes only in the last scene; the title starts "Đúng hay sai #N:". No "Số" labels and no "số ba" loop in this format: the open loop is the verdict.

Visuals:
- visual_query = the subject plus one visible action or setting, 2 to 3 words. No emotions or states ("stressed", "happy"), no two subjects in one query ("asian man yawning dog" found nothing). If the action is rare on stock, use a close-up of the subject. Scene 1 shows the viewer's own kind of pet facing the camera.
- Each scene gets its own visual_query showing that fact's image, and no two scenes share one. Pick things stock video really has: the animal or object in action, close-ups, everyday settings ("cat stretching", "cat eyes close up", "cat sleeping sofa"). If a fact is about an inside part (heart, brain, blood), show the whole animal doing something instead.
- Name the right species in every query: a video about human eyes must say "human eye blink close up", never just "eye blink" (stock returned cats). Add "person" or "woman/man" for human subjects, and say what the camera sees in the last scene too: a still dark photo for the final 8 seconds makes viewers swipe, so ask for a moving clip ("person looking in mirror", "close up eye pupil").

Truth:
- State only facts a reference work states plainly. If something is disputed or unexplained, say so instead of resolving it.
- No invented numbers, names, dates, quotes or studies. Prefer a round, well-known figure over a precise one you cannot back.
- Never present a legend or a viral claim as proven ("chín bộ não" is a viral simplification, not a fact).
- sources is required: one line per fact, naming where a reviewer can confirm it, e.g. "Số 1: Encyclopaedia Britannica, mục Cat" or "Số 2: National Geographic, bài về thị giác của mèo". A well-known publication or institution and the page or topic. Never a URL, because invented links look real.
