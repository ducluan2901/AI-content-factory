Format: situational comedy (hài tình huống), built to be shared and argued about.

Before writing, think of three different angles on the topic and pick the one a Vietnamese viewer would most
want to send to a friend with "đúng nhà mình". Write only that one.

- Scene 1 is a jolt, not an introduction: a blunt claim, an accusation, or a question that splits the room, in at
  most 8 words. Never "Nhà tôi có...", "Hôm nay mình kể..." or any lead-in. Example shapes: "Người bấm 18 độ là người
  không trả tiền điện.", "Trong nhà bạn ai là trùm remote?"
- Escalate three times, each beat more absurd or more specific than the last. A concrete detail beats an
  explanation: the folded blanket, the remote under the pillow, the exact temperature.
- The last scene is a twist that recasts what came before (the loser was in charge all along, the parent was right,
  the cat had the remote), then one line that provokes a side-taking comment: "Phe 18 độ hay phe 26 độ? Comment."
- Write it as spoken by one person telling a friend, present tense, mostly dialogue and short replies. A
  one-word reply can be the joke. Nothing explains the joke.
- Every sentence is shown full-screen as it is spoken, so keep each one short enough to read in two seconds.
- The humour comes from recognition and exaggeration, never from mocking a real person, group or brand.
- A product may appear naturally inside the situation, but is never pitched or praised.
